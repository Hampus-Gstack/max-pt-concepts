00What this is

A repeatable machine that turns Max’s week into 5–6 posts a week without Max ever having to “think of content”. Four parts:

  1. Format library

    16 proven content formats, reverse-engineered from the 7 accounts Max sent us, then rebuilt for his persona (ex-military, close protection, tattooed, calm) and his client (women 40+).

    Section 03

  2. Shooting log

    One card per piece of content: hook, beats, shot list, location, duration. Max films what the card says. Nothing else.

    Section 05

  3. Weekly shooting schedule

    One 90-minute film block per week, batch-filmed. Sent Sunday evening.

    Section 06

  4. Content calendar

    Cursus edits, captions, schedules and reports. Max only films.

    Section 07

Division of labour

Max films. Cursus does everything else.

The pattern is not subtle.

Follower count does not predict engagement. The three accounts with a strong point of view and a personal story (Paul, Shane, Xavier) get 2–4% of their audience engaging on a median post. The three accounts posting generic coach tips (Jason S., Jason B., Gary) get 0.04–0.13% — one-tenth to one-hundredth as much, despite bigger audiences. Gary’s account is the cautionary tale: templated “coach for men 40+” content with meme-style hooks, listicles and “DM me READY” CTAs, and a median of 12 likes on 21K followers.

What actually performed, across all seven

  • Story beats tips.Origin stories, “then vs now”, personal history. Harry’s reintroduction post is his best post of the year. Paul’s “every diet I’ve tried in 20 years” did 3.6K / 353 comments.
  • Contrarian lists explode the comments.Paul’s two “things that need to go” reels: 25.9K / 1.7K and 3.2K / 448. The CTA is always “What have I missed?” — the audience finishes the list.
  • Purpose beats aesthetics.Shane trains to surf; his surf-trip reel (17.3K) outperforms every workout demo. Training for a life, not a body.
  • Simple evidence, one action.Shane’s “muscle loss starts in your 30s and speeds up after 40” — 3.6K / 108 as a plain photo post.
  • Follow-along routines get saved.Shane’s 20-minute “morning activation” with reps in the caption: 7.6K / 171.
  • Personality and humour.Harry’s military anecdote clip (waking up drunk before an 8-mile march): 3.1K / 55.
  • A daily consistency engine.Xavier’s “Daily Morning Minute Message” — one thought, 60 seconds, to camera. It’s the format that keeps the account alive between the big posts.
  • Comment-keyword lead gen works.Harry’s “Comment COACHING and I’ll DM you”: 794 / 30 on a low-effort reel.
What did not work Tips without a person behind them, generic hooks (“Does this describe you?”), client results as abstract graphics, and anything that looks like a template.
02

Principles · six

Principles for Max’s content

  1. He is the format.

    A tattooed ex-close-protection officer who is the calmest, most patient person in the room is a story no other trainer in Stockholm can tell. Every piece should be un-copyable by someone without his background.

  2. Protector, never drill sergeant.

    Close protection is about preparation, calm, and making the day boring for the client. That is the lens: “I plan so nothing goes wrong. That’s what your program is.”

  3. Speak to her, not at her.

    The audience is women 40+. Address the fears we heard in Haven’s research: intimidating gyms, diet-culture fatigue, “am I too old”, “will I get hurt”, “where do I even start.”

  4. Strength, never shrinking.

    No before/after bodies, no scale numbers, no “summer body”. Proof is deadlifts, bone density, blood pressure, carrying grandkids, hiking.

  5. One idea per piece.

    If a script needs a second point, it’s two pieces.

  6. Batch, don’t improvise.

    One film block a week produces the whole week. Improvised content is what kills consistency.

Guardrails · six rules

Guardrails

  1. No before/after body photos. No scale numbers. No “summer body”, “toned”, “shred”, “bikini”.

  2. No client identifiable without written consent; first name + age only.

  3. No medical claims. Facts are cited in the caption. Never mention HRT or supplements by brand.

  4. Military: lessons and calm, never combat stories, never anything that dramatises violence.

  5. Tone: calm, dry, kind. Max never shouts, never points at the camera, never uses the word “ladies”.

  6. Every piece must pass one question — below.

Every piece must pass one question
Would a nervous 52-year-old feel safer after watching this?
